Foaming agents & SIP formwork systems

An integrated transfer of formulation, equipment, and construction know-how for lightweight cellular concrete and monolithic building systems.

Every engagement starts with four questions: what the concrete will be used for, the output you need, which cement and sand are available locally, and what the project has to satisfy. Foaming-agent dilution and cement-to-sand ratios are never fixed in advance. Each recipe is measured and qualified through trial mixes, and generator capacity, pressure and flow are confirmed against equipment data and commissioning measurements.

Production then runs as one continuous workflow, from foam generation through mixing, transfer, placement and inspection. Foam density, fresh concrete density, workability and material consumption are logged on a repeatable sampling plan, and curing, demoulding and loading follow approved project criteria. SIP formwork stays in place as permanent formwork and is not dismantled: walls are cast monolithically around door and window frames and embedded services. Any contribution to insulation or cladding is designed and verified for the specific assembly.

Foaming Equipment

A generator configured around the production line.

A foaming system brings together a prepared solution, controlled water supply, compressed air, foam generation, and the downstream mixing sequence. Whether a plant is arranged for continuous operation or discrete batches depends on the generator design, required output, available utilities, and the process that is commissioned.

In continuous production, an appropriately engineered system can feed foam at a controlled rate into a compatible mixing line. Batch operation instead allows a defined quantity of foam or mix to be produced and checked between cycles; the selected arrangement should match the target volume, site logistics, and quality-control procedure.

Technical Process

Three capabilities, one production system

Every project is scoped around the same inputs: intended use, target output, local material sources, available utilities and acceptance criteria. Formulation, foam generation, mixing, transfer, placement and inspection are planned as one workflow, not as separate operations.

No dilution ratio or cement-to-sand ratio can be fixed in advance. The recipe is established from product data, the mix design and the materials on site, then qualified through trial mixes before production release. A generator delivers steady foam only inside its verified operating envelope, with output matched to mixing and placement; capacity, pressure and flow are confirmed with equipment data and commissioning measurements. Pump and hose performance follow the mix, the route, the lift and the rate of placement.

Foam density, fresh concrete density, workability, material consumption and interruptions are recorded on a repeatable sampling plan. Placed material is protected, and curing, demoulding, loading and follow-on trades follow approved project criteria and strength evidence; curing time varies with formulation, geometry, temperature and humidity. In SIP assemblies the formwork is never removed, so the wall arrives already formed and insulated, with any insulation or cladding contribution designed and verified for that assembly.

Qualify the process

Set project acceptance targets, document measured formula inputs, and trial selected cementitious materials before production.

Balance foam output

Match verified generator delivery to mixer demand and placement rate; check air, water, and pressure against machine documentation.

Commission with evidence

Record density, consumption, interruptions, curing conditions, and test results to build repeatable operating procedures.
