Casting System

System-Integrated Panel Formwork

SIP means Stay-In-Place. The panels are permanent formwork that is not dismantled after the concrete is placed: they are never struck, stripped or returned to the yard. It serves as structural insulation formwork with a finished mould surface: concrete fills the cavity, the assembly cures as one monolithic wall, and the panel face remains as the finished interior or exterior surface.

See the Casting a House Method

The value of formwork that stays

Because the formwork is never removed, the wall arrives already formed and already insulated. There is no stripping cycle, no cleaning, no transport back to the yard and no re-erection on the next level. The material is bought once and stays in the building.

The effect on programme and cost is direct: fewer site hours per square metre, fewer crane lifts, less waste, and a faster move from one floor to the next. The wall is complete when the concrete sets.

Technical Process

From set-out to finished wall, in one sequence

Every project is scoped around the same inputs: intended use, target output, local material sources, available utilities and acceptance criteria. Formulation, foam generation, mixing, transfer, placement and inspection are planned as one workflow, not as separate operations.

No dilution ratio or cement-to-sand ratio can be fixed in advance. The recipe is established from product data, the mix design and the materials on site, then qualified through trial mixes before production release. A generator delivers steady foam only inside its verified operating envelope, with output matched to mixing and placement; capacity, pressure and flow are confirmed with equipment data and commissioning measurements. Pump and hose performance follow the mix, the route, the lift and the rate of placement.

Foam density, fresh concrete density, workability, material consumption and interruptions are recorded on a repeatable sampling plan. Placed material is protected, and curing, demoulding, loading and follow-on trades follow approved project criteria and strength evidence; curing time varies with formulation, geometry, temperature and humidity. In SIP assemblies the formwork is never removed, so the wall arrives already formed and insulated, with any insulation or cladding contribution designed and verified for that assembly.

Coordinate before the pour

Check panel joints, bracing, reinforcement, openings, conduits, supports, and structural approvals.

Place under controlled conditions

Use qualified mix proportions and a planned sequence; monitor form movement, leakage, pressure, and fresh density.

Retain the useful formwork

Permanent formwork avoids a stripping operation and may contribute insulation or cladding when designed and verified for those functions.
